Wick — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 October 2026

Wick turns a television into a care surface for the person being cared for. It is built on a simple commitment: the resident is the user, not the subject. This policy describes exactly what Wick collects, what it shares, and what it refuses to do.

Who we are

Wick is operated by Roger Bavibidila, Zurich, Switzerland. Contact: github.com/rogerjeasy/wick/issues.

What Wick collects

  • Doorbell and camera events from a Ring device the household has connected — that motion or a button press occurred, and a still image used momentarily to describe who is at the door.
  • Television activity — when the TV is on, what is playing, and when. Used to learn the household's ordinary rhythm.
  • Interactions with Wick — which cards were shown and which buttons were pressed.

What Wick never does

  • No camera ever shows the inside of the home to anyone. Ring is used for the front door and for the fact that someone arrived or left. It is never used to watch the resident.
  • No verbatim conversation ever leaves the household. Raw speech is discarded on equipment in the home; only derived facts travel.
  • No mood, sentiment or emotional inference is produced or reported to anyone.
  • No location tracking of the resident.
  • No wellness score. Wick writes sentences, not ratings.
  • No sale of personal data, and no advertising.

What is shared with family

A written policy, enforced in software, governs every sentence that reaches a family member. Information about health, finances, relationships or location is withheld by default. Household rhythm and commitments are shared only as summary, never as raw logs. The resident sees their own weekly narrative before any family member can read it, and can permanently remove anything from what is shared using a single button on their own television.

What Wick holds about a family member

A family member signs in with an email address and a password, or tries Wick as a guest with neither. Wick keeps:

  • Your account: your name and email. Your password is kept by Amazon Cognito; Wick never stores it.
  • Your place in each family circle: the name the person at home sees, your role, and whether they have allowed you.
  • What you sent: notes and their replies, pictures (and whether she has seen each), invitations, visits you said you would make, a television you connected as your own.
  • Sign-ins: the tokens the family app holds, and the browsers you asked to be told on. Only a digest of each token is stored.

How long data is kept

  • Door images are discarded within 24 hours, enforced by storage policy rather than by application code.
  • Sign-ins: an access token lasts one hour, a refresh token and the sign-in page's cookie 30 days. Signing out of the family app ends both tokens and the cookie at once.
  • Invitations expire after 7 days.
  • Pictures: a picture she lets go of is deleted within a day; one never answered, after about 13 months; one she keeps, when she takes it down. A reading of a card aloud is kept one day.
  • Your account is kept until you delete it.
  • Backups: the database keeps point-in-time backups for 35 days, so anything deleted is gone from them 35 days later.

Events and derived facts are retained for 90 days by default and can be set lower. Rhythm baselines are statistical and contain no content.

Download your data, or delete your account

In the family app, press Account (or say “my account”):

  • Download my data gives you a JSON file of everything above that Wick holds about you. It contains no password, code or token.
  • Delete my account asks for your password again (a guest has none), names what goes, and then deletes at once: your account and your Cognito sign-in, every token and sign-in on every device, the browsers kept to tell you, your place in each circle, the visits you announced and any television you connected as your own.

A home is never left without the person who set it up. If you set up a home, deleting your account closes it, and the app asks you to choose that in so many words first. Closing a home disconnects its Ring doorbell and its televisions (the resident's shows its pairing code again), removes everyone from its circle, and deletes everything Wick holds about it — its notes, pictures, weeks and history. If the doorbell cannot be disconnected, nothing is deleted and you are asked to try again.

A note or a picture you sent to a home somebody else set up was given to the person who lives there: it stays with that home, and is deleted when that home is closed. It is listed in your download.

Where data is processed

Amazon Web Services. Conversational context is processed on equipment inside the household; only derived facts are transmitted.

Your rights

The resident may withdraw at any time, for any reason, without explanation. Withdrawal deletes everything — events, derived facts, rhythm baselines, stored media and agent memory. It is a single action and is not negotiated. Family members can download what Wick holds about them, and delete their account, themselves in the family app (above); they may also ask for correction through the contact address.

Children

Wick is not designed for or directed at children and does not knowingly collect information about them.

Changes

Material changes will be published here and surfaced on the television before taking effect.
