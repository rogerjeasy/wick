The idea

Most care technology is built for the caregiver. Wick is built for her.

People over 75 watch more than four hours of television a day (BLS, 2025). Wick meets them there, and asks them to install nothing, learn nothing and wear nothing.

Built for the caregiver

  • A phone app full of alerts, checked from far away
  • Cameras and sensors that watch her
  • Wearables she forgets to charge
  • Decisions made about her, not with her

Built for her

  • Calm cards on the television she already uses
  • The front door only; nothing inside is watched
  • One button on the remote she already holds
  • Nothing is shared until she says yes

How it works

Three things, done quietly.

The door

A ring puts a card on her television in seconds, then the doorbell's picture and one plain sentence about who is there. Even over another app.

Ring Partner API · Amazon Nova Lite

The family

Her daughter asks Alexa+ "How's Mum been this week?" and gets an honest answer. Notes wait for a natural break; she replies with one press.

MCP server · Claude Sonnet 4.5 on Bedrock

The rhythm

Wick learns what a normal week looks like in her home, so it stays silent when nothing is wrong, and asks her first when something is.

Features

Twelve features. Each one says what it will never do.

Every feature is one button for her, and every one has a rule that protects her, enforced in code.

Ask Sarah to look

One press asks her daughter to look at the same doorbell picture.

Never shows the door unless she asks.

Shall I tell Sarah?

An unusual day is put to her first, with the exact sentence.

Never tells what she keeps to herself.

Calm points

Notes wait for the credits, a pause, or a quiet cut measured in the film.

Never interrupts a scene.

Watch together

Her daughter's screen follows hers, from miles away.

Never lets anyone touch her remote.

The light in the window

The family sees one thing: whether her day has begun.

Never says when, where or what.

Hold for Sarah

Hold OK for two seconds to ask one person to ring her.

Never calls anyone or raises an alarm.

The visit, witnessed

After the carer leaves: "Is this right?"

Never confirms a visit without her.

Sarah's television

Her daughter's own Fire TV shows her light and requests.

Never becomes her television.

Her language

Spanish, the moment she says yes on her own screen.

Never machine-translates her consent.

The mantelpiece

Family pictures she can keep on her idle screen.

Never tells whether she kept one.

Read to me

Play reads the card aloud, in her language.

Never speaks unless she asks.

Easier to see

Larger text and higher contrast, everywhere at once.

Never shrinks a word to make it fit.

Privacy and trust

Privacy is the product, not a policy page.

No camera inside her home

Ring is used at the front door, and for the fact that someone arrived. Never to watch her.

Her consent, on her screen

Nobody reads her home until she presses Allow, checked again on every request.

Some things never leave

Health, money, relationships and location never reach the family. A rules engine checks every word first.

No guessing about feelings

No mood detection, no wellness scores. Wick reports what happened, never what it thinks she felt.

Architecture

The television shows. The cloud thinks. The family reads through a gate.

Serverless on AWS, all in Terraform, deployed from GitHub over OIDC.

Her television

Vega app or Fire OS build. Renders and reports; decides nothing.

Ring doorbell

Signed webhooks, recorded frames, live view.

Wick on AWS

IoT Core and API Gateway at the edge. Agents on Lambda and Bedrock AgentCore. One DynamoDB table.

The Guardian

A rules engine on every word bound for the family. No model decides what is private.

The family

Alexa+ over a standard MCP server, with OAuth 2.1.

Sarah's television

Her own view, and nothing more.
